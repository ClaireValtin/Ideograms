How to read this, and its limits

  • Breakpoints sit in the middle of the band you pick. ISCN places a break "in" a band without saying where inside it, so the drawing uses the band's midpoint. Pick 400 or 550 bands to match the resolution of your preparation; ISCN karyotypes use the band level actually resolved, e.g. t(9;22)(q34;q11.2).
  • Band names you can type. Any band or sub-band name from the 400, 550 or GRCh38 (~700) idiogram, or a parent band such as 22q11.2 (the break then sits in the middle of its sub-bands). p10 and q10 are the centromere.
  • Ordering follows ISCN 2024 section 4.3. Normal sex chromosomes first (an abnormal X or Y is left out of the sex complement, e.g. 46,X,t(X;13)), then sex-chromosome abnormalities, then autosomes in numerical order, numerical before structural for the same chromosome. Sex-chromosome gains and losses are written constitutionally (47,XXY; 45,X), not as acquired +X/–Y.
  • What is not supported. Insertions, dicentrics, markers, three-way translocations, inverted duplications, mosaics and clone counts. For neoplasia, add the cell count in square brackets yourself, e.g. [20].
  • Derivatives are drawn from the band data. Each piece keeps its own bands, so a translocated segment shows the banding of the chromosome it came from; the coloured strip beside each chromosome shows which chromosome each part comes from.
  • The microscope view uses the simulator engine, with the same unverified choices for band intensity and heterochromatin tone described on the simulator page.
